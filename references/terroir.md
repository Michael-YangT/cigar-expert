# Growing Regions & Leaf Behaviour

Why a region's leaf behaves as it does. The bridge between a spec sheet's wrapper value and a flavour group - see references/flavour-groups.md.

## Andullo
*/ an-DOO-yo / | Growing & Terroir*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/andullo/>`
- **Definition**: A Dominican tradition in which cured leaves are compressed into a dense rope or log, bound and wrapped in royal palm bark , and left to ferment slowly for months or years. Andullo is traditionally shaved off for pipe or chew rather than rolled into cigars.
- **Why it matters**: The technique shows how sustained pressure and slow fermentation transform leaf, and a few makers now blend shaved andullo into premium cigars for its sweet, funky depth.
- **In context**: "A sliver of andullo in the filler gave the cigar an unusual sweet, fermented note."

## Black Shank
*Growing & Terroir*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/black-shank/>`
- **Definition**: A soilborne disease caused by the water mold Phytophthora nicotianae that rots the stalk base and roots, blackening the stem and wilting the plant. It persists in soil for years and spreads with standing water.
- **Why it matters**: Black shank resistance is a main reason growers abandoned classic Corojo and Criollo for modern hybrids, and it drives crop rotation and drainage practice on every serious farm.
- **In context**: "After two seasons of black shank the farm rotated the field out of tobacco entirely."

## Blue Mold (Moho Azul)
*/ MO-o ah-SOOL / | Growing & Terroir*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/blue-mold/>`
- **Definition**: A fast-moving downy mildew caused by Peronospora tabacina, which forms bluish gray spores on leaf undersides and can strip a field within days in cool, damp weather. A devastating outbreak hit Cuba around 1979 and 1980.
- **Why it matters**: That epidemic ended Corojo 's reign and forced the breeding program that produced Criollo 98 , Corojo 99 , and Habano 2000 , so blue mold shaped the flavor of modern cigars.
- **In context**: "One humid week and blue mold took a third of the field."

## Cibao Valley
*/ see-BOW / | Growing & Terroir*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/cibao-valley/>`
- **Definition**: The broad fertile valley in the northern Dominican Republic that runs through Santiago and Villa Gonzalez, and the country's main tobacco region. Piloto Cubano , Olor, and San Vicente all grow here, and Santiago holds most Dominican factories.
- **Why it matters**: Nearly every Dominican premium cigar involves Cibao leaf, Cibao labor, or both, making the valley the Caribbean counterpart to Esteli .
- **In context**: "The whole operation, fields, barns and factory, sits within an hour of each other in the Cibao."

## Condega
*/ kon-DEH-ga / | Growing & Terroir*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/condega/>`
- **Definition**: A Nicaraguan growing region between Esteli and Jalapa , in the department of Esteli. Its leaf tends toward the middle ground, with more body than Jalapa and more sweetness and elegance than Esteli.
- **Why it matters**: Condega tobacco is the connective tissue in many Nicaraguan puros, providing body without the sharp edges that pure Esteli filler can bring.
- **In context**: "A Condega viso filled the gap between the Jalapa seco and the Esteli ligero."

## Connecticut River Valley
*Growing & Terroir*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/connecticut-river-valley/>`
- **Definition**: The tobacco district straddling the Connecticut and Massachusetts stretch of the river, with sandy loam left by glacial floodplain. It produces two very different crops: shade-grown Connecticut wrapper under cheesecloth and sun-grown Broadleaf.
- **Why it matters**: It is the only large-scale premium wrapper region in the United States, and its high land and labor costs are the reason Ecuadorian Connecticut now dominates the market.
- **In context**: "Driving north from Hartford you can still see the white shade tents that gave Connecticut Shade its name."

## Conuco
*/ koh-NOO-koh / | Growing & Terroir*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/conuco/>`
- **Definition**: The Taino method of raised-mound agriculture, in which tobacco was cultivated alongside cassava, maize and sweet potatoes on mounds of soil built to prevent erosion and preserve fertility.
- **Why it matters**: Conuco farming shows tobacco was already a deliberately cultivated, mound-grown crop in the Caribbean well before European contact, not a wild plant that colonists happened upon.
- **In context**: "The tobacco grown on a conuco differed from modern varieties, believed to have been a wild or semi-domesticated strain with smaller leaves and a more potent flavor."

## Corojo Field Practice
*/ ko-ROH-ho / | Growing & Terroir*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/corojo-field-practice/>`
- **Definition**: The intensive regime historically used for Cuban Corojo wrapper : shade cloth, tight plant spacing, heavy fungicide programs, careful topping , and picking two leaves at a time with cotton-gloved hands to avoid bruising. Leaves were sewn in pairs and hung immediately.
- **Why it matters**: The labor bill explains why wrapper costs many times what filler does, and why Corojo 's disease vulnerability eventually made the practice untenable in Cuba.
- **In context**: "Growers describe old Corojo field practice as farming a crop that fought you every week of the season."

## Cosecha
*/ ko-SEH-cha / | Growing & Terroir*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/cosecha/>`
- **Definition**: The harvest, and by extension the crop year. In the northern-hemisphere cigar belt, seed goes into beds in late summer or autumn, plants are transplanted in the dry season, and picking runs from roughly January into April.
- **Why it matters**: Cosecha years matter because weather varies: a rainy or storm-hit crop can affect a blend three or four years later, when that leaf finally reaches the rolling table.
- **In context**: "The 2018 cosecha was wet, and you could taste the thinner ligero in the cigars that eventually used it."

## Cuban Denomination of Origin
*Growing & Terroir*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/cuban-denomination-of-origin/>`
- **Definition**: The protected designation system that reserves terms such as Habano and Vuelta Abajo for tobacco grown and cigars made in defined Cuban zones under specified methods. Habanos S.A. administers the marks, which are recognized in most markets outside the United States.
- **Why it matters**: It is why non-Cuban makers must say Habano seed rather than Habano, and why the word on a box carries a legal claim rather than a stylistic one.
- **In context**: "The denomination of origin covers not just where the leaf grew but how the cigar was rolled."

## Esteli
*/ es-teh-LEE / | Growing & Terroir*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/esteli/>`
- **Definition**: Nicaragua's principal tobacco city and growing region, written Esteli with an accent on the final syllable. Its dark, mineral-rich volcanic soil produces the country's strongest and most peppery leaf, and the city itself holds most of Nicaragua's major factories.
- **Why it matters**: Esteli ligero is the engine behind the full-bodied Nicaraguan style, and its concentration of factories makes the town the practical capital of non-Cuban cigar production.
- **In context**: "The ligero came from Esteli, which is exactly where the black pepper in the finish came from."

## Finca
*/ FEEN-ka / | Growing & Terroir*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/finca/>`
- **Definition**: A farm or rural estate. In cigar country a finca usually includes fields, curing barns, and worker housing under one ownership, and premium producers often name their fincas on packaging.
- **Why it matters**: Vertically integrated makers who own their fincas control seed, curing, and fermentation , which is why single-farm claims are worth checking.
- **In context**: "The whole blend came off one finca in Jalapa, barn included."

## Harvest Passes
*Growing & Terroir*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/harvest-passes/>`
- **Definition**: The sequence of trips through a field in which pickers take two or three leaves per plant, starting at the bottom and working up as each priming ripens. A crop typically requires five to eight passes spread over a month or more.
- **Why it matters**: Because each pass takes leaf of a different character, the harvest schedule is what physically creates the volado , seco , and ligero grades a blender later uses.
- **In context**: "By the fifth pass they were into the centro gordo and the leaves were noticeably heavier."

## Jalapa
*/ ha-LAH-pa / | Growing & Terroir*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/jalapa/>`
- **Definition**: A valley in Nicaragua's Nueva Segovia department, near the Honduran border, with reddish sandy soil and higher elevation than Esteli . Its tobacco is the sweetest and most floral of Nicaragua's main zones.
- **Why it matters**: Blenders use Jalapa leaf to round off the power of Esteli filler , and a blend leaning on Jalapa will usually read medium and aromatic rather than punishing.
- **In context**: "The Jalapa seco brought cedar and sweetness where the Esteli ligero brought pepper."

## Jamastran
*/ hah-mas-TRAHN / | Growing & Terroir*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/jamastran/>`
- **Definition**: A valley in the El Paraiso department of southeastern Honduras, near Danli, with hot conditions and rich soil. Jamastran produces some of Honduras's strongest and most full-flavored filler .
- **Why it matters**: When a Honduran blend tastes heavy, earthy, and sweet at once, Jamastran leaf is often responsible.
- **In context**: "The Jamastran ligero made the cigar noticeably heavier than the Talanga version."

## Los Rios / Quevedo
*/ los REE-os / keh-VEH-do / | Growing & Terroir*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/los-rios-quevedo/>`
- **Definition**: The Ecuadorian coastal province and town at the center of that country's wrapper industry. Persistent cloud cover off the Pacific filters sunlight for much of the growing season, giving shade-like conditions without cloth.
- **Why it matters**: This natural shading, plus rich alluvial soil, is why Ecuador supplies a large share of the world's premium wrapper at a lower cost than tented farms.
- **In context**: "Quevedo grows Connecticut and Habano seed side by side under the same natural cloud cover."

## Oriente
*/ oh-ree-EN-teh / | Growing & Terroir*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/oriente/>`
- **Definition**: The traditional name for eastern Cuba, where tobacco is grown on a modest scale in the provinces around Santiago and Holguin. The leaf is generally strong and rustic and is used domestically rather than in export Habanos.
- **Why it matters**: The region shows up in cigar history more than in cigar blends, and any claim that a premium Habano is built on Oriente leaf deserves scrutiny.
- **In context**: "Oriente tobacco largely feeds the local market rather than the export factories."

## Partido
*/ par-TEE-do / | Growing & Terroir*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/partido/>`
- **Definition**: A small Cuban zone south of Havana, historically the island's premier shade- wrapper district, centered around San Antonio de los Banos. Urban expansion and disease have shrunk plantings drastically from their peak.
- **Why it matters**: Partido wrapper is prized and now scarce, so its decline is part of why Cuba leans on Vuelta Abajo for wrapper as well as filler .
- **In context**: "Old catalogs bragged about Partido wrapper the way modern ones name Ecuador."

## Remedios
*/ reh-MEH-dee-os / | Growing & Terroir*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/remedios/>`
- **Definition**: A central Cuban growing region, part of the broader Vuelta Arriba, spanning parts of Villa Clara and Sancti Spiritus. Its leaf is robust and full flavored but coarser than Vuelta Abajo tobacco, and it goes mostly into cigarettes and lower-tier cigars.
- **Why it matters**: Remedios is the historic heart of Cuban tobacco, predating Pinar del Rio's rise, and its leaf still occasionally shows up as filler in machine-made and domestic brands.
- **In context**: "Remedios was growing tobacco commercially long before Vuelta Abajo took the crown."

## San Andres Veracruz
*/ san an-DRESS veh-ra-KROOS / | Growing & Terroir*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/san-andres-veracruz/>`
- **Definition**: The San Andres Tuxtla valley in Veracruz, Mexico, one of the oldest continuously farmed tobacco zones in the Americas. Its Negro San Andres leaf is thick and dark and is used chiefly as maduro wrapper and as flavoring filler .
- **Why it matters**: San Andres has become the default maduro wrapper for many non-Cuban brands because it ferments to a deep color while keeping an earthy, cocoa sweetness.
- **In context**: "The San Andres wrapper on that robusto gave it a dark cocoa and soil character from the first inch."

## Semi Vuelta
*/ SEH-mee VWEL-ta / | Growing & Terroir*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/semi-vuelta/>`
- **Definition**: A Cuban growing zone in central and eastern Pinar del Rio, adjacent to Vuelta Abajo but on heavier soils. Its plants produce larger, coarser, stronger leaf used mainly for filler and for the domestic cigarette and machine-made trade.
- **Why it matters**: Semi Vuelta rarely appears in top-grade Habanos, so hearing it named is a signal about which tier of production a cigar belongs to.
- **In context**: "Most Semi Vuelta leaf never leaves the island in premium form."

## Semillero
*/ seh-mee-YEH-ro / | Growing & Terroir*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/semillero/>`
- **Definition**: The seedbed where tobacco is germinated before it goes to the field, traditionally a raised, sterilized bed under cloth and now often float trays in shallow water. Seedlings spend roughly six to eight weeks there.
- **Why it matters**: Nearly every disease problem that shows up later can be seeded here, so growers sterilize soil and cull weak plants aggressively before transplanting .
- **In context**: "The semillero was already sown in September so the field would be ready by November."

## Suckering (Desahijado)
*/ des-ah-ee-HAH-do / | Growing & Terroir*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/suckering/>`
- **Definition**: Removing the axillary shoots, called hijos or suckers, that a topped plant pushes out at every leaf joint. It is done by hand every few days, sometimes assisted by chemical growth inhibitors.
- **Why it matters**: Skipped suckering wastes the gains from topping , since the shoots draw off exactly the energy meant for the leaves.
- **In context**: "Crews walked the rows twice a week just to pull suckers."

## Sun Grown
*Growing & Terroir*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/sun-grown/>`
- **Definition**: Tobacco cultivated in open field without shade cloth. Full sun thickens the leaf, deepens color, raises oil and nicotine, and coarsens the veins .
- **Why it matters**: Sun-grown leaf supplies almost all filler and binder , and sun-grown wrapper trades the silkiness of shade leaf for more flavor and grip.
- **In context**: "The sun-grown Corojo wrapper had visible veins and a much heavier aroma than the shade leaf beside it."

## Talanga
*/ ta-LAHN-ga / | Growing & Terroir*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/talanga/>`
- **Definition**: A valley north of Tegucigalpa in Honduras's Francisco Morazan department, at higher elevation and cooler than Jamastran . It is known for wrapper and for more refined, aromatic filler .
- **Why it matters**: Talanga is the counterweight to Jamastran in Honduran blending , contributing finesse rather than force.
- **In context**: "Their Talanga wrapper reads floral where the Jamastran filler reads earthy."

## Tapado
*/ ta-PAH-do / | Growing & Terroir*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/tapado/>`
- **Definition**: Shade growing, from the Spanish for covered. Fields are tented with cheesecloth on posts and wire, which diffuses light, raises humidity, and holds heat, producing thinner, larger, more elastic leaves with finer veins .
- **Why it matters**: Nearly all premium wrapper other than broadleaf and naturally shaded Ecuadorian leaf is grown tapado, and the cloth adds substantial cost per hectare.
- **In context**: "Under tapado the plants stretch for light, so you get more primings and thinner leaf."

## Terroir
*/ tair-WAHR / | Growing & Terroir*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/terroir/>`
- **Definition**: The combined effect of soil, climate, altitude, water, and local growing practice on the character of a crop. In tobacco it explains why one seed variety produces different leaf in Jalapa than in Esteli or Pinar del Rio.
- **Why it matters**: Terroir is why seed names alone tell you little; the same Habano seed can yield a sweet, floral wrapper in Ecuador and a peppery one in Nicaragua.
- **In context**: "He blamed the terroir, not the seed, for how differently the two farms' Corojo smoked."

## Topping (Desbotonado)
*/ des-bo-toh-NAH-do / | Growing & Terroir*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/topping/>`
- **Definition**: Cutting off the flower head once the plant is mature, so energy that would go into seed goes into the remaining leaves. The height at which a grower tops sets how many primings the plant will carry.
- **Why it matters**: Topping directly raises leaf thickness, oil, and nicotine, and topping low concentrates strength while topping high yields more but lighter leaf.
- **In context**: "They topped the field at eighteen leaves, keeping it light because the crop was destined for wrapper."

## Transplanting
*Growing & Terroir*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/transplanting/>`
- **Definition**: Moving seedlings from the semillero into the field once they are roughly six to eight inches tall and have a workable root system. Plants are set in rows by hand, watered in, and spaced according to whether the crop is destined for wrapper or filler .
- **Why it matters**: Spacing set at transplanting decides leaf size and how much sun each plant gets, so it is one of the earliest decisions that shapes the finished cigar.
- **In context**: "They transplanted after the first rains, then irrigated by furrow for the next two weeks."

## Vega
*/ VEH-ga / | Growing & Terroir*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/vega/>`
- **Definition**: A tobacco field or plantation, especially in Cuba, where the word carries the sense of a specific named plot with its own reputation. Vegas are identified by soil type, drainage, and the family that works them.
- **Why it matters**: Buyers of Cuban leaf trade on vega names much as wine buyers trade on vineyards, and factories pay premiums for leaf from particular plots.
- **In context**: "The best wrapper on the truck came from a single vega in San Juan y Martinez."

## Vega Fina
*/ VEH-ga FEE-na / | Growing & Terroir*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/vega-fina/>`
- **Definition**: A top-rated tobacco plot, the Cuban designation for a vega whose leaf consistently qualifies for the highest grades. In Cuba only a limited number of plots in Vuelta Abajo hold this standing.
- **Why it matters**: Leaf from a vega fina is where the scarce grades come from, including the wrapper and medio tiempo that go into limited releases.
- **In context**: "Only a handful of vegas finas in San Luis supply wrapper for the top marcas."

## Veguero
*/ veh-GEH-ro / | Growing & Terroir*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/veguero/>`
- **Definition**: A tobacco farmer, specifically one who works a vega . In Cuba the veguero is a distinct social figure, often working land his family has farmed for generations under quota agreements with the state.
- **Why it matters**: The veguero makes the choices, on topping height, picking timing, and barn management, that decide how good the leaf can possibly be before a factory ever sees it.
- **In context**: "The veguero decided to hold the corona priming an extra week, betting on more oil."

## Vuelta Abajo
*/ VWEL-ta ah-BAH-ho / | Growing & Terroir*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/vuelta-abajo/>`
- **Definition**: The westernmost tobacco zone of Cuba's Pinar del Rio province, centered on San Juan y Martinez and San Luis. Its sandy, iron-rich soils and reliable dry season make it the only Cuban region that produces all leaf classes, wrapper included, at the top grade.
- **Why it matters**: Essentially every premium Habano draws on Vuelta Abajo leaf, and the region's small size is a permanent ceiling on Cuban production.
- **In context**: "Ask where the wrapper came from and the answer, for any serious Habano, is Vuelta Abajo."
