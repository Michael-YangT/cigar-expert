# Drink & Food Pairing / 酒食搭配

Pairing guidance anchored to the cigar's own character. Written against the
flavour groups in `flavour-groups.md` rather than against brand names, because a
brand says nothing about what is in the glass.

## The governing principle

**Match intensity, then match sweetness, then stop.** Two rules do most of the
work:

1. Neither side should drown the other. A delicate Connecticut Shade disappears
   under a cask-strength bourbon; a Nicaraguan puro flattens a light lager into
   water.
2. Sweetness in the drink cools the palate and makes the cigar read **milder**;
   high alcohol and dryness make it read **harsher**. This is why a sweet rum
   rescues a strong cigar and a dry martini ruins a mild one.

Pair by the cigar's body and its wrapper group — not by its country, and not by
its price.

Water is listed on purpose. It is the correct answer more often than people
think, and it is what belongs between a cigar and anything else — a palate
carrying bourbon cannot taste a light cigar at all.

## Wrapper group overlay

Body alone is not enough; the wrapper decides the aroma, and aroma is what
actually meets the drink. Combine the matrix above with the group from
`flavour-groups.md`:

| Group | What it does in a pairing | Lean towards |
|---|---|---|
| Light (Connecticut Shade, Claro) | Delicate, easily overwhelmed | Dry white, pilsner, light tea, water |
| Neutral (Habano, Corojo, Sumatra) | Takes most things | Bourbon, dry sherry, coffee |
| Cuban | Earthy, distinctive, wants company that does not compete | Aged rum, cognac, mild tea |
| Dark fermented (Maduro, San Andrés, Broadleaf) | Sweet, dense, stands up to power | Espresso, aged rum, peated Scotch, dark chocolate |
| Chinese domestic | Often lighter and sweeter than expected | Light tea, lager, water |

The counter-intuitive case worth stating out loud: **a maduro that tastes strong
pairs with more, not less.** Fermentation sweetness gives it the structure to
stand beside peated whisky, while a mild-bodied light wrapper pairs with almost
nothing but water and dry wine.

## Food

Food pairing matters less than drink pairing, because cigars are usually eaten
*after* food. Where it comes up:

- **Dark chocolate (70%+)**: the most reliable match for maduro and for coffee
  forward cigars. Fat and bitterness both carry.
- **Nuts — 杏仁、核桃、烤腰果**: neutral, resets the palate, works at any body.
- **Aged hard cheese**: stands up to full-bodied cigars; soft fresh cheese does
  not pair with anything here.
- **Grilled red meat**: conventional and defensible, mainly because of the fat.
- **Citrus, 橙皮**: the traditional cleaner between cigar and drink.

Avoid strong spice and vinegar with anything light — the cigar will not survive
the contact.

## Order and technique

1. Drink first, then smoke, then drink again — the sequence that shows what each
   did to the other. Most people get this backwards.
2. Work from mild to strong across a session. A strong cigar first makes
   everything after it taste like paper.
3. Rinse with water between drinks. Sweet drinks especially leave a film that
   mutes the next third of the cigar.
4. Do not serve anything ice-cold and expect it to pair. Numbing the palate is
   not a pairing.

## What not to do

- **Do not pair by brand.** There is no drink that goes with a name.
- **Do not recommend a specific bottle, bar, shop or price.** Pairing advice ends
  at the category. This skill gives no retail or purchasing guidance.
- **Do not claim a pairing is canonical.** There is no authority for it; say what
  works and why, and let the user disagree.
- **Do not ignore the storage state.** A cigar stored too wet tastes flat, and no
  pairing fixes that — check `defect-diagnosis.md` before blaming the whisky.
