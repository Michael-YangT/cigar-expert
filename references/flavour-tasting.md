# Flavour, Strength & Tasting Terms

Strength, body and finish. This is what answers how strong a cigar reads and how it sits alongside a drink.

## Ammonia
*/ uh-MOHN-yuh / | Flavor & Tasting*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/ammonia/>`
- **Definition**: A sharp, acrid smell and taste from nitrogen compounds released during tobacco fermentation . Its presence in a finished cigar indicates the leaf has not fully rested.
- **Why it matters**: Ammonia is the clearest sign that a cigar needs more time in the humidor , and it usually dissipates with months of rest rather than requiring the cigar to be discarded.
- **In context**: "There was ammonia on the cold draw, so the box went to the back of the cabinet for a year."

## Balance
*Flavor & Tasting*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/balance/>`
- **Definition**: The degree to which a cigar's components support rather than dominate one another, so that no single note, nor the strength , overruns the rest.
- **Why it matters**: An unbalanced cigar can be full of interesting flavors and still be unpleasant, which is why experienced tasters weigh balance as heavily as complexity .
- **In context**: "Plenty of pepper, but the balance kept it from turning into an attack."

## Black Pepper
*Flavor & Tasting*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/black-pepper/>`
- **Definition**: A tasting descriptor for a sharp, prickling spice, felt as much as tasted, and usually most pronounced on the retrohale . Frequently attributed to ligero and to Nicaraguan tobacco.
- **Why it matters**: Pepper often dominates the first inch and then recedes, so judging a blend on the opening puffs alone tends to mislead.
- **In context**: "The pepper was fierce for an inch, then settled into something rounder."

## Blind Tasting
*Flavor & Tasting*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/blind-tasting/>`
- **Definition**: Evaluating cigars with the band removed or concealed, so that brand, price, and reputation cannot influence the assessment.
- **Why it matters**: Blind evaluation is the standard control against expectation bias and is the reason major publications de- band samples before scoring them.
- **In context**: "Blind, the inexpensive Nicaraguan outscored two of the marquee names."

## Body
*Flavor & Tasting*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/body/>`
- **Definition**: The weight, density, and mouthfeel of the smoke, described on a scale from light to full. Body is a textural judgment, independent of how much nicotine a cigar delivers.
- **Why it matters**: Confusing body with strength is the most common beginner error in tasting notes, and separating the two makes descriptions far more useful.
- **In context**: "Medium body, but the strength crept up past the band."

## Cedar
*Flavor & Tasting*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/cedar/>`
- **Definition**: A tasting descriptor for the aromatic, slightly sweet, resinous woodiness associated with Spanish cedar . It can come from the tobacco itself or be absorbed from box and humidor lining.
- **Why it matters**: Because storage contributes to it, cedar is one of the notes that most clearly reflects how and how long a cigar was kept.
- **In context**: "Years in the cabinet had pushed the cedar to the front."

## Cocoa
*/ KOH-koh / | Flavor & Tasting*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/cocoa/>`
- **Definition**: A tasting descriptor for unsweetened chocolate or cocoa powder, dry rather than confectionary. A frequent note in Ecuadorian and Mexican wrappers and in many maduro blends.
- **Why it matters**: Cocoa is one of the most reliably shared descriptors between tasters, which makes it a useful anchor when calibrating a palate against published reviews.
- **In context**: "Dry cocoa on the finish, not sweetness."

## Coffee
*Flavor & Tasting*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/coffee/>`
- **Definition**: A tasting descriptor covering roasted coffee bean and espresso character, sometimes with a bitter edge. Common in darker, oilier wrappers .
- **Why it matters**: Coffee notes often intensify as a cigar heats up in the final third, so their arrival can indicate cadence as much as blend.
- **In context**: "Espresso showed up right where the pepper dropped away."

## Complexity
*Flavor & Tasting*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/complexity/>`
- **Definition**: The number and interplay of distinct flavors a cigar presents, and how they change over the course of the smoke. A complex cigar rewards attention; a simple one does not require it.
- **Why it matters**: Complexity is what separates a memorable cigar from a merely pleasant one, and it is the quality most blenders are chasing when they layer multiple tobaccos.
- **In context**: "Three tobaccos, and the complexity showed in every transition."

## Cream
*Flavor & Tasting*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/cream/>`
- **Definition**: A tasting descriptor for a smooth, rounded, dairy-like texture and flavor, often reported alongside nuts or bread. Associated with Connecticut shade and other lighter wrappers .
- **Why it matters**: Creaminess is as much a mouthfeel judgment as a flavor one, which is why it appears in notes about body as often as in notes about taste.
- **In context**: "Creamy and nutty throughout, a proper morning cigar."

## Earth
*Flavor & Tasting*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/earth/>`
- **Definition**: A tasting descriptor for mineral, soil, mushroom, or forest floor character. Common in Cuban tobacco and in many Nicaraguan blends.
- **Why it matters**: Earth is a foundational note in a great many premium blends and functions as the base layer that sweeter or spicier flavors sit on top of.
- **In context**: "A damp earth core ran under the whole cigar."

## Finish
*Flavor & Tasting*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/finish/>`
- **Definition**: The flavors that remain on the palate after the smoke is exhaled, and how long they persist. Described by both length and character.
- **Why it matters**: A long, clean finish is a mark of good tobacco, and a short or bitter one often reveals underfermented leaf that the initial impression concealed.
- **In context**: "The finish ran long, drying, with cocoa underneath."

## Leather
*Flavor & Tasting*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/leather/>`
- **Definition**: A tasting descriptor for a supple, tanned, faintly sweet character, sometimes further specified as saddle leather or new leather. Often paired with earth and cocoa .
- **Why it matters**: Leather is a hallmark of well-fermented, mature filler tobacco and is one of the notes aging tends to bring forward.
- **In context**: "Leather and cocoa carried the last third."

## Nicotine Sickness
*/ NIK-uh-teen / | Flavor & Tasting*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/nicotine-sickness/>`
- **Definition**: The acute effects of absorbing more nicotine than the smoker is accustomed to: nausea, sweating, dizziness, headache, and a racing pulse. Colloquially called being nicked or getting sick.
- **Why it matters**: It is the most common unpleasant experience for new smokers, and it is worth stating plainly that cigars deliver nicotine and carry established health risks including cancers of the mouth, throat, larynx, esophagus, and lung, regardless of whether the smoke is inhaled.
- **In context**: "He smoked a full-strength double corona on an empty stomach and paid for it."

## Palate Fatigue
*/ PAL-it / | Flavor & Tasting*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/palate-fatigue/>`
- **Definition**: The dulling of taste and smell perception over a long smoke or across several cigars, as receptors adapt to sustained stimulus.
- **Why it matters**: Fatigue explains why the final third of a big cigar often reads as monotonous, and why serious tasting sessions are kept short and spaced with water.
- **In context**: "By the third cigar palate fatigue had flattened everything into generic wood."

## Retrohale
*/ REH-troh-hale / | Flavor & Tasting*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/retrohale/>`
- **Definition**: Exhaling a portion of smoke through the nose so it passes over the olfactory receptors. Most of what people call taste is smell, so this reveals aromas the tongue cannot register.
- **Why it matters**: Retrohaling roughly doubles the detectable flavor in most cigars and is the fastest single improvement a developing palate can make, though it amplifies pepper sharply and should be learned gently.
- **In context**: "Only on the retrohale did the cinnamon show up at all."

## Scoring Methodology
*Flavor & Tasting*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/scoring-methodology/>`
- **Definition**: The system by which a publication or taster converts an evaluation into a number. Most major outlets use a 100-point scale weighting appearance and construction, flavor, smoking characteristics, and overall impression, with the scale in practice compressed into the 80s and 90s.
- **Why it matters**: Understanding the weighting and the compression explains why a 92 and an 88 can be far further apart in practice than four points suggests.
- **In context**: "Their methodology puts half the weight on flavor, which shows in the results."

## Strength
*Flavor & Tasting*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/strength/>`
- **Definition**: The physiological impact of a cigar, driven chiefly by nicotine content in the filler . Rated light, medium, or full, and felt rather than tasted.
- **Why it matters**: Strength determines whether a smoker can finish comfortably, and it tracks poorly with flavor intensity, so a mild-tasting cigar can still be potent.
- **In context**: "It tasted gentle but the strength was full, and he ate first next time."

## Transition
*Flavor & Tasting*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/transition/>`
- **Definition**: A shift in a cigar's flavor profile as it burns, whether gradual or abrupt. Reviewers typically map transitions against the thirds .
- **Why it matters**: Transitions are the strongest evidence that a blend was deliberately constructed rather than assembled from a single tobacco, and they are what keeps a long smoke interesting.
- **In context**: "The transition into the second third brought espresso where there had been hay."

## Vertical Tasting
*Flavor & Tasting*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/vertical-tasting/>`
- **Definition**: Smoking the same cigar from several different production years, or the same blend across several vitolas , in one sitting to isolate a single variable.
- **Why it matters**: Holding everything else constant is the only rigorous way to judge what aging or what size actually does to a blend, rather than relying on memory.
- **In context**: "The vertical ran from a 2011 box to last year's, and the middle years were the best of them."
