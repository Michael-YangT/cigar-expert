# Burn & Structure Defects / 燃烧与结构故障

Diagnosing a cigar that is burning wrong or drawing wrong. Read this before
answering any question of the form "my cigar is doing X, why".

The terms and their causes are Cigar Capitol entries; the handling steps are
practical guidance and are marked as such. Where a source disagrees, both figures
are given — never pick one silently.

## First split: storage cause or construction cause

This is the fork that decides everything downstream, and it is answerable from
one question: **is it one cigar or several?**

| 观察 | 判为 | 处置方向 |
|---|---|---|
| 多支、不同品牌、同期都出问题 | 储存（湿度/温度） | 改环境，不要改手法 |
| 同一盒内多支出同症状 | 该盒/该批 | 整盒隔离并检查湿度 |
| 单支出问题，同盒其他正常 | 卷制（construction） | 单支处置，环境不用动 |
| 每次都出，但只在这一款 | 该型号特性 | 换尺寸或降低湿度再抽 |

Getting this wrong is expensive: people rebuild their whole storage setup over a
single badly rolled cigar, and just as often keep re-lighting an over-humidified
box as if it were a construction fault.

## Canoeing / 斜烧

`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/canoeing/>`
One side runs ahead of the other, hollowing the cigar into a canoe shape. Capitol
names wind, uneven lighting and moisture variation across the leaves as causes.

- **Most likely**: lit unevenly, or smoked outdoors in moving air.
- **Handling** *(practical)*: let it rest; a mild canoe often self-corrects as the
  slower side catches up once the coal evens out. If it does not, touch the flame
  to the lagging side only.
- **Prevention**: toast the whole foot until the rim glows evenly before the first
  draw — an uneven start is the single most common cause.

## Tunneling / 隧道烧

`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/tunneling/>`
The filler burns faster than the wrapper and binder, so the coal recedes into a
tube while the outer leaves lag behind. Capitol also lists it as a symptom of
over-humidified storage.

- **Most likely**: storage RH too high, or an underfilled bunch. Tunneling is the
  classic wet-cigar signature — a tight draw and flat flavour usually come with it.
- **Handling** *(practical)*: purge the cigar gently to push air through, then
  relight. It will not fully recover; the fix is drying, not technique.
- **Prevention**: drop storage RH. Check whether the cigar came from a group whose
  target is 62% — see `flavour-groups.md` — rather than assuming 70% is right.

## Runner / 快烧线

`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/runner/>`
A point that burns faster than the tobacco around it, running up one side. Capitol
attributes it to uneven filler density or a thin patch in binder or wrapper.

- **Most likely**: construction. Unlike canoeing, a runner tends to persist.
- **Handling** *(practical)*: touch the flame only to the slow side, repeatedly if
  needed.
- **Prevention**: none at the smoking end; if a whole box runners, it is a bad box.

## Coning / 锥形烧

The centre burns ahead of the ring, leaving a cone. Common in figurados and in
cigars whose bunch is deliberately looser at the foot.

- **Most likely**: shape and bunch design, not a defect.
- **Handling** *(practical)*: normal; nothing to correct unless the wrapper stops
  burning entirely, which makes it tunneling instead.

## Extinguishing / 熄火

`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/relighting/>`
Goes out between draws.

- **Most likely**: puffed too slowly, or over-humidified, or both.
- **Handling** *(practical)*: knock off the dead ash, purge the stale smoke, toast
  the exposed foot, then draw. Relighting without purging is why a re-lit cigar
  tastes bitter.
- **Prevention**: one draw every 30–60 seconds; slower than that the coal cools.
  If a box keeps going out regardless of pace, it is too wet.

## Plug / 堵塞

`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/plug/>`
A localised blockage in the filler that stops or badly restricts draw. Capitol
names a folded leaf, a piece of stem left in the bunch, or over-compression.

- **Most likely**: construction. Cannot be fixed by storage.
- **Handling** *(practical)*: check the cut first — too small a cut mimics a plug.
  If it is a true plug, massaging the cigar sometimes shifts the fold; a stem plug
  usually does not clear. Do not drill it.
- **Prevention**: none. Note the box; if several are plugged, it is a bad box.

## Tight draw / 吸阻大

`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/tight-draw/>`
Excessive effort to pull air; severe cases are plugged. Causes include overfilled
bunches, a folded filler leaf, or too small a cut.

- **Split**: if the cigar feels firm along its whole length, it is overfilled. If
  it feels firm at one spot only, it is a plug.
- **Handling** *(practical)*: re-cut slightly wider first. Persistent tightness in
  a whole box usually means the box is stored too wet — dry it and retest.

## Open draw / 吸阻过小

`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/open-draw/>`
Very little resistance, high smoke volume, burns hot and fast.

- **Most likely**: underfilled or loosely bunched, sometimes a cheap blend. Also
  the smoking-side symptom of a dried-out cigar.
- **Handling** *(practical)*: slow the pace down. An open draw overheats quickly
  and turns bitter.

## Too dry / 过干

- **Signs**: brittle wrapper, cracks when handled, burns hot and fast, thin
  flavour, wrapper splits at the foot.
- **Handling** *(practical)*: raise RH **gradually** over two to four weeks. Rapid
  re-humidification splits wrappers that were already brittle.
- **Prevention**: check the seal and the pack, not just the gauge.

## Too wet / 过湿

- **Signs**: spongy feel, tight draw, tunneling, flat flavour, goes out often,
  wrapper darkens and can spot.
- **Handling** *(practical)*: dry at the target RH for the cigar's group rather
  than "lower" in the abstract.
- **Prevention**: sort by group and set RH per group — see `flavour-groups.md`.

## Cracked wrapper / 爆衣

- **Most likely**: handled dry, or re-humidified too fast, or cold-to-warm
  transition with condensation.
- **Handling** *(practical)*: a small crack at the foot rarely matters; a long
  split down the body will not hold a burn.
- **Prevention**: never handle a cigar that feels crisp; let it come to room
  temperature in its wrapper before opening.

## Mold vs bloom / 发霉与开花

The single most-asked storage question. **Plume brushes off cleanly and leaves no
mark; mould is raised, patchy, stains the leaf and smells musty.** Full entry and
reasoning: `production-curing.md`.

- Mould: isolate, then wipe and re-store at lower RH; check the neighbours in the
  same container, because they were exposed to the same conditions.
- Plume: nothing to do. Confirm it really is plume before saying so — brushing off
  cleanly is the test.

## Beetle risk / 虫害

Sources disagree on the activation temperature, and this must be reported as a
disagreement rather than resolved: Cigar Capitol states eggs hatch reliably above
roughly **22°C (72°F)**, while other guidance cites **24°C**. Give both and let
the user choose the margin.

- **Signs**: small round exit holes in the wrapper, fine dust at the foot or in
  the box, visible tunnels into the filler.
- **Handling** *(practical)*: isolate the box immediately; treat the whole
  container, not just the affected cigar.
- **Prevention**: temperature control matters more than humidity here. A cabinet
  running at 16–18°C is well below both cited thresholds.

## What not to do

Do not diagnose from a photograph alone. Several of these look alike — tunneling
and canoeing are routinely confused, and a plug cannot be seen at all. Ask which
symptom came first: **a cigar that draws badly from the start is a construction
problem, and one that starts fine then goes wrong is almost always storage.**
