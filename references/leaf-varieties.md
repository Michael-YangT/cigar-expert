# Leaf Varieties & Wrapper Types

Wrapper and filler varieties. This is the primary evidence for assigning a cigar to an odour group, since odour follows wrapper fermentation rather than taste.

## Arapiraca
*/ ah-rah-pee-RAH-ka / | Tobacco & Leaf*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/arapiraca/>`
- **Definition**: Brazilian tobacco grown around Arapiraca in the state of Alagoas. Coarser and more robust than Mata Fina , it cures to a very dark brown and is used chiefly as a maduro -style wrapper .
- **Why it matters**: Arapiraca brings dark, earthy sweetness with more grip than Mata Fina , and it is a common alternative when Broadleaf supply is tight.
- **In context**: "They swapped Broadleaf for Arapiraca that year and the cigar came out earthier."

## Barber Pole
*Tobacco & Leaf*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/barber-pole/>`
- **Definition**: A cigar wrapped in two contrasting wrappers cut into strips and applied in an alternating spiral, most often a light natural against a dark maduro . Building one requires cutting and aligning both leaves precisely so the seams stay tight.
- **Why it matters**: Beyond looks, the twin wrappers genuinely alternate on the palate, and the extra labor means barber poles are usually made by the shop's most skilled rollers.
- **In context**: "The barber pole shifted between sweet maduro and dry Connecticut depending on which stripe was burning."

## Besuki
*/ beh-SOO-kee / | Tobacco & Leaf*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/besuki/>`
- **Definition**: Indonesian leaf from the Besuki region of East Java, traditionally sold in the late-harvest Na-Oogst grade for binder and wrapper . Java-grown tobacco of this type is thin, mild, and quick burning, and it underpins much of the Dutch and German cigar trade.
- **Why it matters**: Besuki and other Javan leaf define the taste of European dry-cured cigars, and they show up as binder in premium blends where combustion matters more than power.
- **In context**: "The binder was Java Besuki, which is why the cigar burned like a wick."

## Cameroon
*/ KAM-eh-roon / | Tobacco & Leaf*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/cameroon/>`
- **Definition**: Wrapper grown in Cameroon and the Central African Republic from Sumatra -derived seed, cultivated under natural cloud cover rather than cloth. The leaf is thin, notably toothy, and tan to brown with a distinctive nutty aroma.
- **Why it matters**: Cameroon supply is small and inconsistent, so blends built on it are vulnerable to crop years; its tooth and delicacy also make it fragile in the rolling room.
- **In context**: "That pebbled, sandpapery surface is the giveaway for genuine Cameroon."

## Centro Fino
*/ SEN-tro FEE-no / | Tobacco & Leaf*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/centro-fino/>`
- **Definition**: The upper middle priming , above centro ligero and below centro gordo . Its leaves are fine grained and elastic, often the best material on a shade plant for wrapper .
- **Why it matters**: On tapado wrapper farms, centro fino leaves regularly bring the highest price per leaf because they combine size, thinness, and even color.
- **In context**: "Almost every wrapper in that box came from centro fino, judging by the fine grain."

## Centro Gordo
*/ SEN-tro GOR-do / | Tobacco & Leaf*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/centro-gordo/>`
- **Definition**: Literally the thick center. This priming sits just below the corona and yields large, heavy, well-developed leaves that usually classify as ligero or heavy viso .
- **Why it matters**: Centro gordo is where a plant delivers its best combination of size and strength , so it matters both for filler power and for large wrapper leaves.
- **In context**: "The centro gordo leaves were wide enough to cut two wrappers apiece."

## Centro Ligero
*/ SEN-tro lee-HEH-ro / | Tobacco & Leaf*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/centro-ligero/>`
- **Definition**: The lower middle priming , taken above uno y medio . Despite the name, the leaves are light rather than strong; ligero here means light in weight and color, not the filler grade.
- **Why it matters**: The naming trips people up constantly, and confusing centro ligero with ligero filler leads to misreadings of how a cigar was built.
- **In context**: "He pointed out that centro ligero has nothing to do with ligero strength, it just sits low on the stalk."

## Claro
*/ KLAH-ro / | Tobacco & Leaf*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/claro/>`
- **Definition**: A light tan or golden wrapper shade, typically from shade-grown leaf given gentle fermentation . The related trade term cristal is sometimes used for the palest, glossiest examples.
- **Why it matters**: Claro wrappers usually accompany milder blends, and their pale surface shows every blemish, so growers must be far more selective about which leaves qualify.
- **In context**: "The claro wrapper was so pale that a single water spot would have disqualified the leaf."

## Colorado
*/ ko-lo-RAH-do / | Tobacco & Leaf*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/colorado/>`
- **Definition**: A medium reddish brown wrapper shade, sitting between Colorado Claro and maduro on the traditional color scale. Colorado Maduro is the next step darker.
- **Why it matters**: Colorado wrappers usually signal medium-to-full flavor with pronounced sweetness and cocoa notes, and the shade is common on Cameroon and aged Habano leaf.
- **In context**: "Under the lamp the wrapper read Colorado, a clear step darker than the Colorado Claro next to it."

## Colorado Claro
*/ ko-lo-RAH-do KLAH-ro / | Tobacco & Leaf*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/colorado-claro/>`
- **Definition**: A light reddish brown wrapper shade, one step darker than claro and lighter than colorado . It is the classic tone of well-aged Cuban and Dominican wrappers.
- **Why it matters**: Many smokers treat Colorado Claro as the visual sweet spot, since it usually indicates ripe leaf that has been fermented and aged without being pushed to maduro .
- **In context**: "A tawny Colorado Claro wrapper with a faint sheen usually means the box has some age."

## Connecticut Broadleaf
*Tobacco & Leaf*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/connecticut-broadleaf/>`
- **Definition**: A thick, sun-grown Connecticut variety harvested by cutting the whole stalk rather than by priming . Its heavy, rugged leaf takes long fermentation well and becomes a dark, sweet maduro wrapper .
- **Why it matters**: Broadleaf gives maduro its characteristic dark chocolate and molasses sweetness; the veins are prominent and the leaf is brittle, which raises rolling costs.
- **In context**: "Broadleaf maduro on a rough day in the rolling room means a lot of torn wrappers."

## Connecticut Shade
*Tobacco & Leaf*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/connecticut-shade/>`
- **Definition**: Wrapper grown under cheesecloth tents in the Connecticut River Valley , developed in the early twentieth century from Cuban and Sumatran stock. Shading yields a thin, silky, pale golden leaf with fine veins .
- **Why it matters**: It is the reference point for mild, creamy cigars, and the same seed grown in Ecuador now supplies most of the market because Connecticut land and labor are expensive.
- **In context**: "A true Connecticut Shade wrapper still costs several times what its Ecuadorian equivalent does."

## Corojo
*/ ko-ROH-ho / | Tobacco & Leaf*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/corojo/>`
- **Definition**: A Cuban variety developed in the mid twentieth century at the El Corojo vega in Pinar del Rio and grown under shade as Cuba's standard wrapper for decades. Extreme susceptibility to blue mold and black shank pushed Cuba to retire it in favor of hybrids in the 1990s.
- **Why it matters**: Corojo descendants grown in Honduras and Nicaragua give the spicy, reddish, sun-grown wrappers many smokers associate with the name, which is not the same plant Cuba once shaded.
- **In context**: "The label says Corojo, but in practice that means a Honduran descendant rather than pre-1997 Cuban seed."

## Corojo 99
*/ ko-ROH-ho noh-VEN-ta ee NWEH-veh / | Tobacco & Leaf*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/corojo-99/>`
- **Definition**: A disease-resistant Cuban hybrid released around 1999 to replace classic Corojo as a shade-grown wrapper variety. It keeps much of the parent's leaf structure while tolerating blue mold far better.
- **Why it matters**: Along with Habano 2000 , Corojo 99 is the reason Cuban wrapper supply stabilized after the blue mold years, and it defines the look of modern Habanos wrappers.
- **In context**: "Most of the shade wrapper coming out of San Juan y Martinez now is Corojo 99."

## Corona (Priming)
*/ ko-ROH-na / | Tobacco & Leaf*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/corona/>`
- **Definition**: The topmost regular priming on a sun-grown tobacco plant, harvested last. Corona leaves are small, thick, and strong, and they grade out as ligero in most sorting rooms. The same word also names a cigar size, which is a separate usage.
- **Why it matters**: Knowing that corona is a leaf position, not just a vitola , makes factory talk about primings much easier to follow.
- **In context**: "The corona priming came off the field in early April, weeks after the volado at the base."

## Criollo
*/ kree-OH-yo / | Tobacco & Leaf*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/criollo/>`
- **Definition**: Spanish for native or creole, and the traditional name for the old Cuban filler variety grown in the sun. Historic criollo was used for filler and binder while corojo supplied wrapper ; the original strain has largely been replaced by improved selections.
- **Why it matters**: Criollo lineage sits under a large share of modern Central American filler , so the name signals a flavor family more than a single seed.
- **In context**: "The binder was a Honduran criollo, sun grown and fermented hard."

## Criollo 98
*/ kree-OH-yo noh-VEN-ta ee OH-cho / | Tobacco & Leaf*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/criollo-98/>`
- **Definition**: A Cuban selection released in the late 1990s by the island's tobacco research institute to restore criollo character with better disease resistance, particularly to blue mold . It is grown in the sun for filler and binder and has been widely adopted outside Cuba.
- **Why it matters**: Criollo 98 is a workhorse in Nicaragua and Honduras, and blenders reach for it when they want classic Cuban-style filler that will actually survive a wet season.
- **In context**: "Their Esteli filler is Criollo 98, which explains the pepper on the retrohale."

## Cristal
*/ krees-TAHL / | Tobacco & Leaf*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/cristal/>`
- **Definition**: A trade term for an exceptionally pale, glossy, thin wrapper , essentially the lightest end of the claro range. Usage varies between factories and it has never been a standardized grade.
- **Why it matters**: When you see cristal on a label, expect a delicate shade-grown wrapper and a mild blend, but check the actual leaf origin, since the word carries no fixed meaning.
- **In context**: "They called the wrapper cristal, which in practice meant a very light Ecuadorian Connecticut."

## Double Claro (Candela)
*/ KAHN-deh-la / | Tobacco & Leaf*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/double-claro/>`
- **Definition**: A green wrapper , also called candela or AMS for American Market Selection, produced by harvesting early and curing fast with heat so chlorophyll is locked in rather than broken down. The barn cycle takes days rather than weeks.
- **Why it matters**: Candela dominated the United States market into the 1960s and survives as a niche style; it tastes grassy and sweet and is nearly impossible to fake with ordinary curing.
- **In context**: "The candela's bright green wrapper drew stares, and it smoked with a fresh, tea-like sweetness."

## Ecuadorian Habano
*Tobacco & Leaf*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/ecuadorian-habano/>`
- **Definition**: Cuban-seed wrapper grown in Ecuador's coastal lowlands, where near-constant cloud cover acts as natural shade. The result is a leaf with Habano spice but thinner, more elastic, and more evenly colored than sun-grown Habano elsewhere.
- **Why it matters**: It has become the default premium wrapper for medium-to-full blends because it delivers flavor and rolling reliability at the same time.
- **In context**: "Ecuadorian Habano is now so common that its absence is what gets mentioned on a band."

## Ecuadorian Sumatra
*Tobacco & Leaf*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/ecuadorian-sumatra/>`
- **Definition**: Sumatra -seed wrapper grown in Ecuador under the same natural cloud cover that shades the country's Habano crop. It is thin and fine grained, darker and sweeter than Connecticut Shade but milder than Habano.
- **Why it matters**: This is the wrapper blenders reach for when they want visual darkness and gentle sweetness without adding strength .
- **In context**: "The Ecuadorian Sumatra looked like a maduro and smoked like a medium cigar."

## Habano 2000
*/ ah-BAH-no dos MEEL / | Tobacco & Leaf*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/habano-2000/>`
- **Definition**: A Cuban hybrid, also written Habana 2000, released in the late 1990s for resistance to blue mold and black shank . It is grown both under shade for wrapper and in the sun for filler , and yields more usable leaf per plant than the varieties it replaced.
- **Why it matters**: Higher yield and disease tolerance made it commercially essential, though some old hands argue it trades a little aromatic depth for that reliability.
- **In context**: "That wrapper is Habano 2000, thinner and more even than the Corojo it replaced."

## Habano Seed
*/ ah-BAH-no / | Tobacco & Leaf*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/habano-seed/>`
- **Definition**: A broad label for Cuban-descended tobacco varieties grown outside Cuba, covering everything from Corojo and Criollo lines to their modern hybrids. It describes ancestry rather than a specific cultivar.
- **Why it matters**: Because the term is unregulated, Ecuadorian Habano and Nicaraguan Habano can differ enormously; treat it as a family name and look for the growing region for real information.
- **In context**: "The band says Habano wrapper, so the next question is where it was grown."

## Kentucky Fire-Cured
*Tobacco & Leaf*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/kentucky-fire-cured/>`
- **Definition**: Dark tobacco cured over smoldering hardwood fires in closed barns, a process that drives smoke phenols deep into the leaf. Traditionally grown for snuff and chewing tobacco, it entered premium cigars in the 2010s.
- **Why it matters**: Even a small amount reads unmistakably of campfire and barbecue, so it is used sparingly and polarizes smokers.
- **In context**: "Just one fire-cured leaf in the filler pushed the whole cigar toward smoked meat."

## Libre de Pie
*/ LEE-breh deh pee-EH / | Tobacco & Leaf*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/libre-de-pie/>`
- **Definition**: The bottom priming , literally free of the foot , meaning the leaves nearest the ground. Harvested first, they are thin, sandy, often damaged, and grade as volado .
- **Why it matters**: This priming sets the harvest clock: once libre de pie comes off, the field is on a schedule of passes every few days up the stalk.
- **In context**: "Rain had spattered soil on the libre de pie, so half of it was culled before it ever reached the barn."

## Maduro
*/ ma-DOO-ro / | Tobacco & Leaf*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/maduro/>`
- **Definition**: A wrapper color class, dark brown to nearly black, produced by using thick leaf and giving it longer or hotter fermentation . Maduro is a process and color designation, not a tobacco variety, and it is often labeled SMS in the old market-selection shorthand.
- **Why it matters**: Extended fermentation converts starches and softens harshness, which is why maduro usually tastes sweeter and rounder rather than simply stronger.
- **In context**: "He asked for the maduro, expecting sweetness rather than the extra strength people assume comes with it."

## Mata Fina
*/ MAH-ta FEE-na / | Tobacco & Leaf*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/mata-fina/>`
- **Definition**: Brazilian tobacco from the Reconcavo region of Bahia, named for its growing area. It is dark, oily, and naturally sweet, fermented long and used mostly as wrapper and as a flavoring filler .
- **Why it matters**: Mata Fina supplies a dried-fruit and cocoa sweetness that is hard to get from Cuban-seed leaf, which is why blenders slip it into otherwise Central American filler .
- **In context**: "The Brazilian Mata Fina wrapper gave the blend a raisin note the Nicaraguan filler could not."

## Natural (EMS)
*Tobacco & Leaf*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/natural/>`
- **Definition**: The broad middle of the wrapper color range, from light tan through medium brown, marked EMS for English Market Selection in the old three-part shorthand of AMS, EMS, and SMS. Natural simply means the leaf was cured and fermented conventionally, without candela treatment or extended maduro fermentation .
- **Why it matters**: Most cigars sold today are natural, and the term is best read as a negative definition: not green, not maduro .
- **In context**: "The line comes in natural and maduro, and the natural is the one most shops stock deepest."

## Nicotiana Tabacum
*/ nik-oh-she-AH-na ta-BAH-kum / | Tobacco & Leaf*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/nicotiana-tabacum/>`
- **Definition**: The cultivated tobacco species from which all cigar tobacco comes. A member of the nightshade family, it arose as a natural hybrid of two South American Nicotiana species and exists today as hundreds of named varieties and cultivars.
- **Why it matters**: Every seed name a smoker sees, Corojo , Criollo , Piloto Cubano , Broadleaf, is a variety of this one species, which is why they cross-breed so freely.
- **In context**: "Corojo and Connecticut Broadleaf look nothing alike in the field, yet both are Nicotiana tabacum."

## Olor Dominicano
*/ oh-LOR do-mee-nee-KAH-no / | Tobacco & Leaf*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/olor-dominicano/>`
- **Definition**: A Dominican variety long established on the island, with large, thin, quick-burning leaves. Olor is milder than Piloto Cubano and is used mainly as binder and as a combustion-friendly filler component.
- **Why it matters**: Olor is what keeps many Dominican cigars burning straight, and its mild aroma lets stronger leaf lead without fighting it.
- **In context**: "An Olor binder let the Piloto filler come through cleanly."

## Oscuro
*/ os-KOO-ro / | Tobacco & Leaf*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/oscuro/>`
- **Definition**: The darkest wrapper class, deeper than maduro and often close to black, achieved with the thickest top- priming leaf and the longest fermentation . Some oscuro wrappers are also left on the plant longer before harvest.
- **Why it matters**: Oscuro leaf is heavy and slow burning, so it can dominate a blend and complicate combustion if the filler is not built to match.
- **In context**: "The oscuro wrapper was almost oily to the touch and left a black smudge on the fingers."

## Pelo de Oro
*/ PEH-lo deh OH-ro / | Tobacco & Leaf*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/pelo-de-oro/>`
- **Definition**: An old Cuban variety whose name means hair of gold, celebrated for intense, distinctive flavor and notorious for its vulnerability to blue mold and other disease. Cuba effectively stopped commercial cultivation of it, though growers in Nicaragua and elsewhere have revived small plantings.
- **Why it matters**: It is a collector's leaf: when a blender lists Pelo de Oro, the yield was small and the flavor is meant to be the point.
- **In context**: "A few Nicaraguan farms still put in a hectare of Pelo de Oro and accept that they may lose it."

## Pennsylvania Broadleaf
*Tobacco & Leaf*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/pennsylvania-broadleaf/>`
- **Definition**: Sun-grown seedleaf tobacco from Lancaster County and surrounding Pennsylvania farmland, historically grown for chewing and cigar filler . The leaf is dark, thick, and forceful, with earthy sweetness and high nicotine.
- **Why it matters**: Blenders use Pennsylvania leaf the way they use ligero , as a strength and body component, and occasionally as a rustic maduro wrapper .
- **In context**: "A single leaf of Pennsylvania broadleaf in the filler added weight without changing the aroma much."

## Piloto Cubano
*/ pee-LOH-toh koo-BAH-no / | Tobacco & Leaf*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/piloto-cubano/>`
- **Definition**: The dominant Dominican filler variety, grown from Cuban seed carried to the Cibao Valley around the 1960s and adapted there for decades. It produces a rich, slightly sweet filler with good body .
- **Why it matters**: Piloto Cubano is the backbone of classic Dominican blends, and it is the reason well-made Dominican cigars taste round rather than thin.
- **In context**: "The filler is Piloto Cubano and Olor, a pairing that has defined Santiago blending for forty years."

## Rosado
*/ ro-SAH-do / | Tobacco & Leaf*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/rosado/>`
- **Definition**: A wrapper with a distinctly reddish or rosy cast, from the Spanish for pinkish. It is a description of hue rather than a variety or a formal grade, and it usually comes from Cuban-seed leaf grown in strong sun.
- **Why it matters**: Rosado shading is often taken as a sign of Corojo lineage and a spicy, sweet profile, though the color alone guarantees neither.
- **In context**: "The rosado tint was obvious once the cigar was next to a plain Colorado wrapper."

## San Vicente
*/ san vee-SEN-teh / | Tobacco & Leaf*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/san-vicente/>`
- **Definition**: A Dominican selection derived from Piloto Cubano , developed to improve yield and disease resistance while keeping Piloto's character. It is grown widely in the Cibao Valley for filler and binder .
- **Why it matters**: When a Dominican blend lists San Vicente, expect Piloto-family flavor with slightly cleaner burn and more uniform leaf.
- **In context**: "Their binder switched from Olor to San Vicente the year the crop came in short."

## Sumatra
*/ soo-MAH-tra / | Tobacco & Leaf*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/sumatra/>`
- **Definition**: A tobacco type originating on the Indonesian island of Sumatra and developed under Dutch colonial trade for thin, elastic, fine-grained wrappers . Sumatra seed is now grown in Ecuador, Nicaragua, Honduras, and Brazil as well as Indonesia.
- **Why it matters**: Sumatra wrapper typically reads sweet, slightly cedary, and mild-to-medium, and its thinness makes it a favorite for cigars meant to smoke cool and even.
- **In context**: "An Ecuadorian Sumatra wrapper gave the cigar a soft, faintly sweet edge that Habano seed would not have."

## Uno y Medio
*/ OO-no ee MEH-dee-oh / | Tobacco & Leaf*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/uno-y-medio/>`
- **Definition**: The second priming from the bottom, named one and a half. These leaves are thin and pale, generally sorted into volado or light seco .
- **Why it matters**: Along with libre de pie , uno y medio provides the burn-friendly leaf that keeps stronger primings smoking evenly.
- **In context**: "Uno y medio came off the plant first week of harvest, in the same pass as the last libre de pie."

## Viso
*/ VEE-so / | Tobacco & Leaf*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/viso/>`
- **Definition**: In Nicaraguan and Dominican practice, the filler priming that sits between seco and ligero : fuller than seco, more combustible than ligero, often glossy with oil. Cuban wrapper sorters historically used viso for oily, sheened leaves rather than for a priming.
- **Why it matters**: Viso is the blender's bridge grade, adding body without the burn penalty that heavy ligero brings.
- **In context**: "Dropping one ligero and adding two viso softened the blend without gutting it."
