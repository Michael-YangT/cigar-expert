# Storage & Aging Terms

Storage failures - humidity swings, beetles, plume mistaken for mould. Chinese renderings must paraphrase these definitions, never claim to translate canon.

## Acclimation
*/ ak-luh-MAY-shun / | Storage & Aging*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/acclimation/>`
- **Definition**: The settling period after cigars are moved between environments, during which their internal moisture equalizes with the new storage. Two weeks is a common minimum; a month or more is safer after shipping.
- **Why it matters**: Smoking a cigar that is still equalizing usually produces uneven burn and muted flavor, and it is the most common reason a well-reviewed cigar disappoints on first try.
- **In context**: "Give a shipped box a month to acclimate before you judge it."

## Boveda Packs
*/ boh-VAY-duh / | Storage & Aging*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/boveda-packs/>`
- **Definition**: Sealed two-way humidity control packets containing a saturated salt solution in a gel, sold at fixed RH targets such as 62, 65, 69, and 72 percent. They both release and absorb moisture to hold that setpoint.
- **Why it matters**: Two-way regulation removes most of the guesswork from storage, since the pack corrects overshoot as well as dryness until it hardens and needs replacing.
- **In context**: "He runs 65 percent Boveda packs in everything and replaces them twice a year."

## Cabinet Humidor
*Storage & Aging*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/cabinet-humidor/>`
- **Definition**: A large freestanding or built-in humidor , often with drawers and shelves and sometimes with active electronic humidification and temperature control. Capacities run from several hundred to many thousands of cigars.
- **Why it matters**: Greater thermal mass and a better seal make cabinets far more stable than small boxes, which matters most for long-term aging.
- **In context**: "He moved the aging boxes into the cabinet humidor and left them alone for five years."

## Cigar Beetle
*Storage & Aging*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/cigar-beetle/>`
- **Definition**: Lasioderma serricorne, the tobacco beetle, a small brown insect whose larvae bore through cigars and leave pinholes and dust. Dormant eggs are present in most tobacco and hatch reliably once temperatures rise above roughly 72 degrees Fahrenheit, or about 22 Celsius.
- **Why it matters**: A single hatch can destroy an entire humidor within weeks, which is why temperature control is as important as humidity control in warm climates.
- **In context**: "He found one pinhole in a Churchill and froze the whole box that afternoon."

## Coolerdor
*/ KOO-ler-dor / | Storage & Aging*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/coolerdor/>`
- **Definition**: A picnic cooler repurposed as a humidor . The insulated plastic shell already seals well and resists humidity swings, so only cedar trays and a humidification source need adding.
- **Why it matters**: It is the cheapest way to get genuinely stable storage for a large collection, which is why so many long-term collectors use one despite the lack of elegance.
- **In context**: "Half his inventory lives in a coolerdor in the basement closet."

## Desktop Humidor
*Storage & Aging*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/desktop-humidor/>`
- **Definition**: A tabletop wooden box, typically holding twenty-five to three hundred cigars, with a cedar lining and a passive humidification device. The most common form of home storage.
- **Why it matters**: It is the practical entry point for most collectors, though its seal and its humidity stability are usually inferior to a cooler or a cabinet.
- **In context**: "A fifty-count desktop humidor was more than enough for his weekly rotation."

## Distilled Water
*Storage & Aging*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/distilled-water/>`
- **Definition**: Water with dissolved minerals removed by distillation. It is the standard choice for charging humidification devices.
- **Why it matters**: Tap water leaves mineral scale that clogs foam and beads, and it can carry microorganisms that promote mold inside a sealed humidor .
- **In context**: "Only distilled water goes in the sponge, never tap."

## Dry-Boxing
*Storage & Aging*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/dry-boxing/>`
- **Definition**: Moving a cigar out of the humidor into a dry, unhumidified box or ordinary room air for a day or several days before smoking it.
- **Why it matters**: It is the standard fix for cigars stored on the wet side, and it usually sharpens the burn line and lifts flavors that high moisture was muting.
- **In context**: "Two days of dry-boxing turned a sluggish burner into a clean one."

## Freezing Protocol
*Storage & Aging*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/freezing-protocol/>`
- **Definition**: A staged process for killing beetle eggs and larvae: seal cigars in an airtight bag, rest them in a refrigerator for about a day, move them to a freezer for roughly three days, return them to the refrigerator for a day, then let them come to room temperature before returning them to the humidor .
- **Why it matters**: The gradual temperature steps prevent the wrapper cracking that a straight trip to the freezer causes, and freezing is the only reliable home remedy once an infestation is confirmed.
- **In context**: "The freezing protocol takes the better part of a week but it saved the collection."

## Humidification Beads
*Storage & Aging*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/humidification-beads/>`
- **Definition**: Silica or zeolite media engineered to hold a target relative humidity , charged with distilled water and reusable indefinitely. Common setpoints are 62, 65, and 70 percent.
- **Why it matters**: Beads are refillable rather than disposable, making them cheaper over the life of a large humidor , though they need recharging on a schedule.
- **In context**: "The bead tube had gone clear, so he misted it with distilled water."

## Humidor
*/ HYOO-mih-dor / | Storage & Aging*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/humidor/>`
- **Definition**: A sealed container that holds cigars at a stable relative humidity , usually lined with Spanish cedar and fitted with some form of humidification. Sizes run from travel cases to walk-in rooms.
- **Why it matters**: Cigars are agricultural products that dry out and crack in ordinary room air, so a humidor is the baseline requirement for keeping any collection smokable.
- **In context**: "The humidor had settled at 65 percent and held there all summer."

## Hygrometer
*/ hy-GROM-uh-ter / | Storage & Aging*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/hygrometer/>`
- **Definition**: An instrument that measures relative humidity . Digital units are generally more accurate than the analog spring-and-needle type, but every hygrometer drifts and needs periodic checking.
- **Why it matters**: Every storage decision rests on the number this device reports, so an uncalibrated hygrometer quietly corrupts everything downstream.
- **In context**: "Both hygrometers agreed, which was reassuring until he calibrated them."

## Over-Humidification
*Storage & Aging*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/over-humidification/>`
- **Definition**: Storage at a relative humidity higher than a cigar burns well at, historically the 70 percent half of the old 70/70 rule. Symptoms include a spongy feel, tight draw , tunneling , and flat flavor.
- **Why it matters**: The tradeoff is real rather than a matter of a single correct number: higher RH protects against cracking and suits some full-bodied blends, while many modern smokers find 62 to 68 percent burns better and tastes more defined. Where you land depends on your climate, your blends, and your tolerance for dry-boxing .
- **In context**: "After a season at 72 percent he dropped the cabinet to 65 and the burn problems stopped."

## Relative Humidity
*Storage & Aging*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/relative-humidity/>`
- **Definition**: The amount of water vapor in air expressed as a percentage of the maximum that air can hold at a given temperature. Abbreviated RH.
- **Why it matters**: Because the maximum is temperature-dependent, the same water content reads as a different RH at different temperatures, which is why a humidor moved to a warm room appears to lose humidity.
- **In context**: "At 70 degrees the gauge read 64 percent RH, right where he wanted it."

## Salt-Test Calibration
*Storage & Aging*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/salt-test-calibration/>`
- **Definition**: A method of checking a hygrometer by sealing it with a slurry of table salt and distilled water , which produces a stable 75 percent RH environment at room temperature after several hours.
- **Why it matters**: It is the only cheap way to know whether a hygrometer is telling the truth, and cheap units are often off by five points or more.
- **In context**: "The salt test showed the gauge was reading four points high."

## Seasoning a Humidor
*Storage & Aging*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/seasoning-a-humidor/>`
- **Definition**: Bringing a new humidor 's cedar up to working moisture before storing cigars in it, typically by leaving a high-RH Boveda pack or a dish of distilled water inside for one to two weeks with no cigars present.
- **Why it matters**: Unseasoned cedar pulls moisture out of the cigars themselves, so skipping this step dries out the first batch you store.
- **In context**: "He seasoned the new cabinet for two weeks before a single box went in."

## Spanish Cedar
*Storage & Aging*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/spanish-cedar/>`
- **Definition**: Cedrela odorata, a tropical hardwood unrelated to true cedar , used to line humidors and to make cigar boxes. It absorbs and releases moisture readily, resists insects, and contributes an aromatic note.
- **Why it matters**: Its moisture buffering smooths out humidity swings, and its aroma is one of the flavors long-aged cigars pick up from the box itself.
- **In context**: "The trays were rebuilt in kiln-dried Spanish cedar."

## Tupperdor
*/ TUP-er-dor / | Storage & Aging*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/tupperdor/>`
- **Definition**: An airtight food storage container used for cigar storage, usually with a Boveda pack and a strip of cedar inside. Common for small collections, travel, and quarantine of new arrivals.
- **Why it matters**: The seal on a good plastic container beats most inexpensive wooden boxes, making it a reliable, cheap option and a useful isolation box for suspect cigars.
- **In context**: "New arrivals sit in a tupperdor for two weeks before joining the main stash."

## Wineador
*/ WY-nee-dor / | Storage & Aging*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/wineador/>`
- **Definition**: A thermoelectric wine refrigerator converted to cigar storage, usually refitted with Spanish cedar shelves or drawers.
- **Why it matters**: It is the accessible way to control temperature as well as humidity, which matters in hot climates where beetle risk rises with the thermometer.
- **In context**: "In a Texas summer a wineador is less a luxury than insurance against beetles."
