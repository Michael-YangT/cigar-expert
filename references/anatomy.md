# Cigar Anatomy & Component Terms

The component vocabulary used by spec sheets - wrapper, binder, filler and the priming grades inside the filler.

## Band (Anilla)
*/ ah-NEE-yah / | Anatomy*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/band/>`
- **Definition**: Paper ring around the cigar carrying the brand, normally placed near the head . Gustave Bock is usually credited with popularizing the band in the nineteenth century; its practical purposes were identifying brands and keeping gloves clean.
- **Why it matters**: Bands identify the marca and the line, but the working rule is physical: warm the band before removing it or you will tear the wrapper underneath.
- **In context**: "Leave the anilla on until you have smoked past it, then slide it up, not down."

## Barrel
*Anatomy*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/barrel/>`
- **Definition**: Straight-sided body of the cigar between head and foot . Spec sheets sometimes use body or shank for the same section, and it is the part that carries the band and shows the wrapper seams .
- **Why it matters**: Rolling the barrel gently between the fingers is the standard field test for soft spots, hard plugs , and uneven bunch density .
- **In context**: "Roll it along the barrel and you can feel the void about an inch up."

## Binder (Capote)
*/ kah-POH-teh / | Anatomy*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/binder/>`
- **Definition**: Leaf wrapped around the bunch to hold the filler together and give the cigar its shape before the wrapper goes on. Binders are chosen for tensile strength and combustion rather than beauty, and often come from thicker primings or from leaves rejected as wrapper.
- **Why it matters**: Binder governs how evenly a cigar burns and how well the bunch holds its draw ; a weak binder shows up as a split side or a lopsided burn line .
- **In context**: "We moved to a Mexican San Andres capote to slow the burn down a touch."

## Bunch
*Anatomy*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/bunch/>`
- **Definition**: Filler leaves gathered and folded, then enclosed in the binder , forming the inner cigar before the wrapper goes on. The bunch establishes draw , burn rate, and most of how the blend actually behaves.
- **Why it matters**: Nearly every draw problem, plug , or tunnel begins in the bunch rather than the wrapper .
- **In context**: "The bunch is fine, it is the cap that is tight."

## Cap
*Anatomy*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/cap/>`
- **Definition**: Piece of wrapper leaf applied over the head to seal the end where the wrapper spiral terminates. Caps are cut from wrapper offcuts and fixed with a trace of vegetable gum ; on a well-made cigar the join is nearly invisible.
- **Why it matters**: Cutting past the cap's shoulder is the usual cause of a cigar unraveling, so knowing where the cap ends tells you where to cut.
- **In context**: "Cut just above the shoulder and you leave enough cap to hold the wrapper down."

## Cellophane
*Anatomy*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/cellophane/>`
- **Definition**: Clear sleeve fitted over individual cigars in many non-Cuban boxes. Made from wood pulp, it is slightly permeable, so cigars continue to breathe and age inside it, though more slowly than naked leaf.
- **Why it matters**: Cellophane protects wrappers in a crowded humidor and yellows over years, which serves as a rough visual clue to age.
- **In context**: "The cello has gone golden on these, they have been sitting since 2016."

## Closed Foot
*Anatomy*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/closed-foot/>`
- **Definition**: Foot covered by a small piece of wrapper so no filler is exposed, a Cuban tradition also called a perfecto foot or pie cerrado. Lighting one means burning through wrapper leaf first.
- **Why it matters**: The covered foot gives a concentrated burst of wrapper flavor over the first few puffs and needs a slightly more patient toast.
- **In context**: "It has a closed foot, so let that wrapper catch properly before you draw."

## Cold Draw
*Anatomy*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/cold-draw/>`
- **Definition**: Pull of air taken through the unlit cigar after cutting, also called the dry draw . It reveals both the mechanical resistance of the bunch and the raw, unburned flavors of the filler and wrapper .
- **Why it matters**: A cold draw with no resistance means a loose bunch and probably a hot smoke, while one you cannot move means a plug ; both are better caught before lighting.
- **In context**: "The cold draw is all cocoa and hay, and the resistance is just about perfect."

## Filler (Tripa)
*/ TREE-pah / | Anatomy*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/filler/>`
- **Definition**: Core of the cigar: the leaves bunched inside the binder that supply most of the tobacco volume and the backbone of the blend. Filler is normally a mix of leaf classes and origins balanced for strength , aroma, and combustion.
- **Why it matters**: Filler blend determines strength and much of the flavor arc, while the way those leaves are folded determines whether the cigar draws freely.
- **In context**: "The tripa is three Nicaraguan leaves plus a little Dominican piloto for sweetness."

## Flag Cap
*Anatomy*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/flag-cap/>`
- **Definition**: Cap formed by leaving a tail of wrapper at the head and folding or twisting it over the top instead of applying a separate round piece. The flag can be laid flat, swirled into a spiral, or left standing as a pigtail .
- **Why it matters**: Flag caps are a visual signature of particular rollers and brands, and when laid flat they cut like any other cap.
- **In context**: "He finishes the lancero with a flag cap instead of a casquillo."

## Foot
*Anatomy*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/foot/>`
- **Definition**: End that gets lit, where the filler is exposed in cross section on a standard parejo . Closed and shaggy feet are deliberate variations on the open foot.
- **Why it matters**: Reading the foot tells you about bunch density and filler blend before you light, and an even, well-packed foot is the first sign of a careful roll.
- **In context**: "You can see three distinct leaves at the foot, so it is a three-leaf bunch."

## Foot Band
*Anatomy*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/foot-band/>`
- **Definition**: Band applied at the foot rather than the head , sometimes wrapping over exposed filler . Used for anniversary marks, blend information, or to dress a closed foot .
- **Why it matters**: Foot bands must come off before lighting; the paper and ink are not meant to be burned.
- **In context**: "Take the foot band off first - people forget and light straight through it."

## Head
*Anatomy*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/head/>`
- **Definition**: End that goes in the mouth, finished with a cap and normally the end nearest the band . Also called the mouth end; on a figurado the head is tapered, pointed, or flattened rather than round.
- **Why it matters**: Cutting, draw resistance, and the point at which a cigar starts to run hot are all judged from the head.
- **In context**: "Warm the foot, never the head."

## Length
*Anatomy*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/length/>`
- **Definition**: Distance from foot to head , quoted in inches in the United States and in millimetres in Cuba and most of Europe. Premium cigars run roughly 4 to 9 inches, with 5 to 7 covering the bulk of production.
- **Why it matters**: Length mostly governs smoking time and how hot the cigar gets toward the end, since a longer body keeps the smoke stream cooler for longer.
- **In context**: "It is 6 1/2 by 42, so figure an hour and a quarter."

## Ligero
*/ lee-HEH-roh / | Anatomy*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/ligero/>`
- **Definition**: Filler leaf from the top of the plant, thick, oily, and slow burning after long fermentation . Ligero carries the most nicotine and the most concentrated flavor, and is normally placed at the centre of the bunch .
- **Why it matters**: Ligero content is the main lever for strength , and ligero placed carelessly in the bunch is a frequent cause of uneven burning.
- **In context**: "There are two leaves of Esteli ligero in the core, so keep the pace slow."

## Long Filler
*Anatomy*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/long-filler/>`
- **Definition**: Filler made of whole leaves that run the full length of the cigar, folded rather than chopped. Long filler is the standard for premium handmade cigars and is what the term hecho a mano implies in practice.
- **Why it matters**: Full- length leaves give a smoother draw and a more consistent flavor from light to nub than chopped tobacco.
- **In context**: "Everything in this line is long filler, Nicaraguan puro, no scraps."

## Medio Tiempo
*/ MEH-dyoh TYEM-poh / | Anatomy*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/medio-tiempo/>`
- **Definition**: Two small leaves that sometimes grow at the very crown of a tobacco plant, above the ligero primings , and appear on only a minority of plants. Cohiba Behike is the best known cigar to advertise medio tiempo in its blend.
- **Why it matters**: Medio tiempo is scarce and intensely flavored, which is why it is used sparingly and priced heavily.
- **In context**: "There is medio tiempo in the BHK, which is part of why it costs what it costs."

## Mixed Filler
*Anatomy*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/mixed-filler/>`
- **Definition**: Blend of long- filler leaf and short filler in the same cigar, also sold as medium filler or a Cuban sandwich. Long leaf provides structure and draw while the scrap fills volume at lower cost.
- **Why it matters**: Mixed filler lets a factory sell a hand-rolled cigar at a lower price, but expect a faster burn and less consistency than full long filler .
- **In context**: "These are a Cuban sandwich, mixed filler, and they smoke better than the price suggests."

## Oils
*Anatomy*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/oils/>`
- **Definition**: Visible sheen on a wrapper produced by resins and essential oils that survive fermentation and remain in the leaf. Oily wrappers look lacquered under direct light and generally indicate correct humidification and unhurried curing.
- **Why it matters**: Sheen is a fast read on storage: a dull, chalky-looking wrapper usually means the cigar has been kept too dry.
- **In context**: "Look at the oils on this, it has been sitting at 65 percent for a year and it shows."

## Pigtail
*Anatomy*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/pigtail/>`
- **Definition**: Twisted tail of wrapper left protruding from the head , also called a curly head. Traditional on Cuban culebras and lanceros , it is decorative and can be snapped off with the fingers rather than cut.
- **Why it matters**: A pigtail signals a hand finish and gives you the option of tearing instead of cutting, which some smokers find opens a cleaner draw .
- **In context**: "Twist the pigtail off, do not cut it, and the cap stays tight."

## Priming
*Anatomy*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/priming/>`
- **Definition**: Horizontal level of leaves on a tobacco plant, harvested from the bottom upward over several weeks. Cuban practice names the levels from the ground up as libre de pie , uno y medio , centro ligero , centro fino , centro gordo , and corona ; higher primings take more sun and yield thicker, stronger leaf.
- **Why it matters**: Priming is the best single predictor of a leaf's strength , thickness, and burn rate, and it determines whether a leaf becomes volado , seco , or ligero .
- **In context**: "That is a centro gordo leaf, you can see the thickness in it."

## Ring Gauge
*Anatomy*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/ring-gauge/>`
- **Definition**: Diameter of a cigar measured in 64ths of an inch, so a 50 ring gauge is 50/64 of an inch, just under 20 mm. Cuban catalogues list diameter in millimetres and convert to ring gauge for export markets.
- **Why it matters**: Ring gauge changes the ratio of wrapper to filler and how much heat the coal produces, so one blend smokes noticeably differently at 42 and at 60.
- **In context**: "Same blend, but at 60 ring gauge the wrapper barely registers."

## Seam
*Anatomy*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/seam/>`
- **Definition**: Line where each turn of the wrapper overlaps the previous one as it spirals up the cigar. Tight, flat, nearly invisible seams indicate careful wrapper application on properly humidified leaf.
- **Why it matters**: Raised or gaping seams often mean the wrapper was worked too dry, and they can open up as the burn line reaches them.
- **In context**: "Seams are invisible on this one, you can barely find where the wrapper starts."

## Seco
*/ SEH-koh / | Anatomy*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/seco/>`
- **Definition**: Middle- priming filler leaf, lighter in body than ligero and quicker to burn. Seco supplies aroma and mid-palate flavor and is usually the largest share of a filler blend by volume.
- **Why it matters**: Seco is where much of a cigar's aroma lives, and it moderates the ligero so a blend is more than raw strength .
- **In context**: "It is mostly seco with a single ligero for spine."

## Secondary Band
*Anatomy*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/secondary-band/>`
- **Definition**: Additional band placed below the main band to mark a line extension, limited edition, or vintage. Cuban Ediciones Limitadas carry a black and silver secondary band; many non-Cuban brands use one to distinguish blends within a marca.
- **Why it matters**: The secondary band is often the only quick way to tell two blends apart when the primary band is shared across a whole portfolio.
- **In context**: "Check the secondary band - that is the 2019 Edicion Limitada, not standard production."

## Shaggy Foot
*Anatomy*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/shaggy-foot/>`
- **Definition**: Foot where the filler leaves are left protruding past the wrapper and binder , giving a frayed, tasseled appearance. It is a deliberate finish , not a fault.
- **Why it matters**: A shaggy foot lights instantly and delivers pure filler flavor before wrapper and binder join the burn, though it is fragile in a pocket.
- **In context**: "The shaggy foot catches fast, you barely need to toast it."

## Shank
*Anatomy*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/shank/>`
- **Definition**: Straight section of a cigar's body , used most often for the untapered portion of a figurado between the head taper and the foot . Usage varies by factory, and some specification sheets treat shank simply as a synonym for barrel .
- **Why it matters**: On a torpedo or piramide the shank is where ring gauge is actually measured, since the taper is not representative of the cigar's diameter.
- **In context**: "It is 52 at the shank, tapering to about 40 at the head."

## Short Filler
*Anatomy*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/short-filler/>`
- **Definition**: Chopped or scrap tobacco used as filler , often the trimmings left over from cutting long-filler cigars. Also called picadura, it burns faster and hotter than whole leaf and is typical of machine-made cigars and inexpensive bundles.
- **Why it matters**: Short filler is not automatically bad, but it burns quicker, draws less evenly, and tends to loosen and flake as the cigar shortens.
- **In context**: "It is short filler in a homogenized binder, which is why it costs two dollars."

## Shoulder
*Anatomy*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/shoulder/>`
- **Definition**: Curved transition between the flat top of the head and the straight side of the barrel , where the edge of the cap sits. On most parejos the shoulder is a few millimetres below the top of the cap.
- **Why it matters**: The shoulder is the cutting landmark: stay above it and the wrapper stays anchored.
- **In context**: "That cut went below the shoulder, which is why it is unraveling."

## Tooth
*Anatomy*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/tooth/>`
- **Definition**: Fine bumpy texture on a wrapper , visible as small raised dots across the leaf. Tooth comes from oil glands and cell structure and is most pronounced on sun-grown wrappers such as Ecuadorian and Nicaraguan habano and on Cameroon leaf.
- **Why it matters**: Tooth is a varietal and cosmetic signal rather than a defect, and many smokers read a toothy sun-grown wrapper as a promise of richer flavor.
- **In context**: "Look at the tooth on that Cameroon, it is like fine sandpaper."

## Triple Cap
*Anatomy*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/triple-cap/>`
- **Definition**: Cuban-style finish built from three separate pieces of wrapper laid over the head , leaving two visible seam lines plus the cap edge. Many non-Cuban factories imitate the look; some genuinely use three pieces, others build the same appearance from a folded flag.
- **Why it matters**: A clean triple cap signals the roller's skill and helps the head resist unraveling after the cut.
- **In context**: "You can see the three lines under the light, that is a proper triple cap."

## Tubo
*/ TOO-boh / | Anatomy*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/tubo/>`
- **Definition**: Individual tube, usually aluminium, glass, or cedar -lined metal, holding a single cigar. Cuban tubos are typically lined with cedar; most designs slow humidity exchange but are not airtight.
- **Why it matters**: Tubos protect a cigar in a jacket pocket, but they are not long-term storage - a cigar in an unsealed tubo will still dry out in a dry room.
- **In context**: "Take a couple of tubos for the flight, they will survive the trip."

## Vein
*Anatomy*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/vein/>`
- **Definition**: Raised rib of leaf structure running through the wrapper . Prominent veins burn at a different rate than the surrounding lamina and are cosmetically undesirable, which is why wrapper is de-stemmed and graded for fine veining.
- **Why it matters**: A thick vein crossing the burn line is a common cause of an uneven burn or a small tunnel.
- **In context**: "That heavy vein is going to hold the burn up on one side."

## Volado
*/ voh-LAH-doh / | Anatomy*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/volado/>`
- **Definition**: Filler leaf from the lowest primings , thin and mild with modest flavor but excellent combustion. Volado is included chiefly to make the bunch burn evenly.
- **Why it matters**: Blends short on volado often burn badly no matter how good the rest of the leaf is; it is a combustion ingredient more than a flavor one.
- **In context**: "We added a little volado to fix the burn without touching the strength."

## Wrapper (Capa)
*/ KAH-pah / | Anatomy*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/wrapper/>`
- **Definition**: Outermost leaf of the cigar, wound in a spiral from foot to head . Wrapper is the most expensive and most cosmetically demanding component, grown under shade or in full sun and selected for elasticity, fine veining, and even color. Estimates of its flavor contribution vary widely, but most makers put it well above its roughly 5 percent share of the cigar's weight.
- **Why it matters**: Wrapper sets the first impression - appearance, aroma, and a disproportionate share of taste - and a cracked wrapper can ruin an otherwise well-made cigar.
- **In context**: "We lost half a priming to hail, so good capa is going to be tight this year."

## Wrapper Grade
*Anatomy*
`<https://cigarcapitol.com/dictionary/wrapper-grade/>`
- **Definition**: Classification of wrapper leaf by size, color, texture, and freedom from damage, assigned at the escogida and refined at the rezagado bench. Systems differ by country and factory: shade-grown Connecticut is sorted into numbered grades, while Cuban wrapper is sorted into named color and size classes before it reaches a roller.
- **Why it matters**: Grading is the reason cigars from one box look alike, since only a small fraction of any wrapper crop is clean enough to face outward.
- **In context**: "We buy the top two wrapper grades and the rest goes to binder."
